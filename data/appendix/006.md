## Eight Gaṇas (Syllabic Feet)

In the case of metres regulated by syllables each line is divided into groups of three syllables each, called gaṇas or syllabic feet, named as @deva[म], @deva[न], @deva[भ], @deva[य], @deva[ज], @deva[र], @deva[स], and @deva[त]. The names and the Schemes of these are given in the following stanza.[^1]

### Mnemonic Verse

@deva[मस्त्रिगुरुस्त्रिलघुश्च नकारो भादिगुरुः पुनरादिलघुर्यः ।]
@deva[जो गुरुमध्यगतो रलमध्यः सोऽन्त्यगुरुः कथितोऽन्त्यलघुस्तः ॥]

That is: @deva[म] has all its syllables long; @deva[न] has all its syllables short; @deva[भ] has its first syllable long, @deva[य] has its first syllable short; @deva[ज] has its middle syllable long; @deva[र] has its middle syllable short; @deva[स] has its last syllable long and @deva[त] has its last syllable short.[^2]

### Symbolic Notation

The symbol ∪ stands for a short or light syllable; the symbol — denotes a long vowel or a heavy syllable. Symbolically represented these Gaṇas will stand as under:

| Gaṇa | Pattern | | Gaṇa | Pattern |
|------|---------|---|------|---------|
| @deva[म] | — — — | | @deva[ज] | ∪ — ∪ |
| @deva[न] | ∪ ∪ ∪ | | @deva[र] | — ∪ — |
| @deva[भ] | — ∪ ∪ | | @deva[स] | ∪ ∪ — |
| @deva[य] | ∪ — — | | @deva[त] | — — ∪ |

Similarly the letter @deva[ल] (la) is used to denote a short syllable and @deva[ग] (ga) a long one at the close of a line.

[^1]: Vide Vṛttaratnākara I. 9. @deva[वर्णः संयोगपूर्वश्च तथा पादान्तगोऽपि वा ॥] Chhandomañjarī.

[^2]: The following verse is easier to remember than one given above; @deva[आदिमध्यावसानेषु यरता यान्ति लाघवम् । भजसा गौरवं यान्ति मनौ तु गुरुलाघवम् ॥]

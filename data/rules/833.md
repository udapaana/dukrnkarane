## Ablative Case: Motion Away From (@deva[अपादान])

The principal sense of the Ablative case is @deva[अपादान] or 'motion away from';[^1] hence the noun from which the motion, real or conceived, takes place is put in the Ablative case; @deva[ग्रामादायाति] 'comes from a village;' @deva[धावतोऽश्वात्पतति] 'falls down from a running horse'; @deva[सदाचारात् भ्रंशते].

**(a)** Words having the sense of @deva[जुगुप्सा], @deva[विराम] or 'cessation,' 'pause,' and @deva[प्रमाद] 'swerving from,' also govern the Ablative case;[^2] @deva[पापात् जुगुप्सते] 'he hates sin;' @deva[न नवः प्रभुराफलोदयात्स्थिरकर्मा विरराम कर्मणः] (Rag. VIII. 22) 'the new monarch, steady in action, did not desist from efforts until they bore their fruit'; @deva[धर्मात्प्रमाद्यति] 'swerves from duty'; @deva[स्वाधिकारात्प्रमत्तः] (Meg. I.) 'careless, failing in the discharge of the duties (of his office)'; so @deva[धर्मान्मुह्यति]; @deva[प्रसमीक्ष्य निवर्तेत सर्वमांसस्य भक्षणात्] (Manu Smr. V. 49.), &c.

@deva[मद्] with @deva[प्र] in the sense of 'to be careless about' is used with the Loc. also; @deva[अतोऽर्थान्न प्रमाद्यन्ति प्रमदासु विपश्चितः] । (Manu Smr. II. 213) on this account wise men are not careless about their wives.

[^2]: @deva[जुगुप्साविरामप्रमादार्थानामुपसंख्यानम्] । Vārt.

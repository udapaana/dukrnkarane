## Formation of Desiderative Base by Reduplication

The Desiderative base is formed by reduplicating the root or base according to the general rules of reduplication given at @ref[444]-@ref[449] and @ref[549] (a) (b), and by adding @deva[स्] (which may by changed to @deva[ष्] according to the rules of Sandhi) to it. The @deva[अ] of the reduplicative syllable is changed to @deva[इ].

**Example:**
@deva[पठ्]—@deva[पपठ्]—@deva[पिपठ्] + @deva[स] = @deva[पिपठिष्] by the following rule.

@note[type=note]{The @deva[स्] of a primitive root is not changed to @deva[ष्] when the characteristic @deva[स्] is changed to @deva[ष्]; @deva[सि]—@deva[सिसीष्], @deva[सिच्]—@deva[सिसिक्ष्] (@deva[क] + @deva[ष]); @deva[स्मि]—@deva[सिस्मयिष्]; @deva[सु]—@deva[सुषूष्]; but @deva[स्था]—@deva[तिष्ठास्]; @deva[सावय्] cau. of @deva[सू]—@deva[सुषावयिष्]; @deva[स्तु], however, forms @deva[तुष्टूषति].}

@note[type=note]{A root takes a Desiderative form only when the agent of the wish and of the action expressed by the root is the same; so @deva[शिष्याः पठन्तु इति इच्छति गुरु] and not @deva[पिपाठयिषति]; also the sense of the root must be the object of the wish: @deva[गमनेन इच्छति] cannot, therefore, be equal to @deva[जिगमिषति].}

@note[type=note]{Though the Desiderative form of a root is not to be found often used in classical language in its character of a verb, yet nominal and participal derivatives from it are not uncommonly to be met with.}

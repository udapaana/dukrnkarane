The most important of these conjunctions and the one very frequently used is @deva[च]. It can never stand first in a sentence; nor can it be used like 'and' in English. It is used with such of the words or assertions it connects or is placed after the last of the words or assertions it joins together; @deva[रामश्च लक्ष्मणश्च] or @deva[रामः लक्ष्मणश्च]; @deva[कामश्च जृम्भितगुणो नवयौवनं च] love with its excellences expanded, and fresh youth; @deva[कुलेन कान्त्या वयसा नवेन गुणैश्च तैस्तैर्विनयप्रधानैः] ।

(a) Sometimes this particle has a disjunctive force; @deva[शान्तमिदमाश्रमपदं स्फुरति च बाहुः]—the hermitage is tranquil yet my arm throbs.

(b) Rarely this particle is used in the sense of 'if'; @deva[जीवितुं चेच्छसि मूढ हेतुं मे गदतः शृणु] oh fool, if you wish to live, &c.

(c) Sometimes it is used as an expletive; @deva[भीमः पार्थस्तथैव च] ।

(d) Sometimes it is used to connect a subordinate fact with a main one; @deva[भिक्षामट गां चानय] wander for alms and bring a cow; @deva[कुट्टिनी च शासिता गोपी च नि सारिता कन्दर्पकेतुश्च पुरस्कृतः] the procuress was chastised, the cowherdess was expelled and Kandarpaketu was honoured.

(e) When the particle is repeated it has sometimes the sense of 'on the one hand,' 'on the other hand,' 'and yet,' @deva[क्व च हरिणकानां जीवितं चातिलोलं क्व च निशितनिपाता वज्रसाराः शरास्ते] where, on the one hand, is the extremely frail life of fawns, and where, on the other, are thy arrows hard like adamant and falling sharply; @deva[न सुलभा सकलेन्दुमुखी च सा किमपि चेदमनङ्गविचेष्टितम्] । on the one hand the full moon-faced lady is not easy to obtain and yet there is this unaccountable sport of love.

(f) Sometimes the repetition of @deva[च] shows the simultaneous or undelayed occurrence of two events; @deva[ते च प्रापुरुदन्वन्तं बुबुधे चादिपूरुषः] they reached the ocean and at the same time the primeval Being also awoke.

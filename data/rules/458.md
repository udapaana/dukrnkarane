(a) Roots of more than one syllable, derived roots and roots of the 10th class are always Seṭ.

(b) Of all monosyllabic roots ending in a vowel those given in the following couplet (Kārikā) are Seṭ and the rest Aniṭ.

@line: @deva[वृड्वृम्भ्यां च विनैकाचोऽजन्तेषु निहताः स्मृताः] @deva[॥] :@

[^1]: The following couplet gives the endings of these roots and their number in each:— @deva[कचछजा दधनपा नमशाः षसहाः क्रमात्] @deva[।] (the second line of the couplet, giving the numerals, is illegible in the page scan and is therefore not transcribed.) The first line gives the ending consonants and thus enables the students to know at once which root is Seṭ and which is Aniṭ; e. g. @deva[कुट्] may be at once known to be a Seṭ root as @deva[ट्] does not occur in the first line. The second line gives the number of roots ending in a particular consonant. Thus roots ending in @deva[क] are @deva[क] i. e. one (@deva[क] being the first consonant) in number; roots ending in @deva[च] are @deva[च] i. e. 6, (@deva[च] being the 6th consonant); roots ending in @deva[छ] are @deva[क] i. e. one, in number, and so on.

[^2]: These couplets, as well as those bearing on the 2nd variety of the Aorist, are composed by Mr. Chintāmaṇa Ātmārām Kelkar, the present learned Śāstri at the Poona Training College.

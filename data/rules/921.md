[^1] With interrogatives the Present is often used in the sense of the Future when thought or desire is implied; @deva[किं करोमि] i. e. (@deva[करिष्यामि]), @deva[क्व गच्छामि] i. e. (@deva[गमिष्यामि]) What shall I do? Whither shall I go? @deva[एतयोः कतरं] or @deva[एतेषां कतमं भोजयसि] (i. e. @deva[भोजयिष्यसि] or @deva[भोजयितासि]) which of these persons will you feed? so @deva[कं नु पृच्छामि दुःखार्तः], &c.; but @deva[कः ग्रामं गमिष्यति]@deva[।]

(a) It is also used in the sense of the Future in conditional sentences as implying a condition and the fulfilment of the desired object; @deva[योऽन्नं ददाति] (@deva[दाता दास्यति वा]) @deva[स स्वर्गं याति] (@deva[याता यास्यति वा]) he, who offers (or will offer) food, goes (or will go) to heaven i. e. if one offers &c.

[^1]: @deva[किंवृत्ते लिप्सायाम्। लिप्स्यमानसिद्धौ च।] Pāṇ. III. 3, 6. 7.
